The idea

A border-crossing line, without forcing the full loop.

Start in Les Contamines, run east through France and Italy, cross the Grand Col Ferret into Switzerland, then step straight into a waiting vehicle at La Fouly. The next morning, finish above Chamonix at Lac Blanc.

This shape is for friends who already know long mountain days and want something beautiful, social and logistically supported without joining a fully guided tour. Bag movement, lodging, route files and reserved transfers would do the heavy lifting; we would still be responsible for pacing, navigation, weather calls and mountain safety.

Proposal, not a booking. No accommodation, transfer or operator has been reserved. Distances, climbing, timings, route GPX, August 2027 availability and prices all need verification.

Why this version?

More of the best. Less of the grind.

This is not universally “better” than completing the Tour du Mont Blanc. It is a better fit if our priority is memorable running, recovery and time together—not collecting the full-loop finish.

What the ≈100-mile circuit adds

The northern and western arc.

  • La Fouly → Champex: the lower Swiss Val Ferret and lakeside village.
  • Champex → Trient: Bovine’s balcony or the harder Fenêtre d’Arpette variant.
  • Trient → Chamonix: Col de Balme, Posettes and the approach along the Aiguilles Rouges.
  • Les Houches → Les Contamines: Col du Tricot beneath the Bionnassay Glacier.

What we still keep: all three countries, Bonhomme, Col de la Seigne, the Italian balcony and Grand Col Ferret, followed by a separate Lac Blanc finale. What we give up is the continuous circle and roughly 34–43 net miles versus the published ≈99-mile version; exact mileage depends on the final variants.

Five running days

The preliminary stage plan.

These are planning targets, not measured courses. Exact start points and route variants must be reconciled into one verified GPX set before anyone books.

  1. 01
    France · Les Contamines → Les Chapieux

    Over the Bonhomme.

    Target: 15–17 mi · +5,000–6,000 ft · 5–7 hr

    Climb through Notre-Dame de la Gorge toward Col du Bonhomme and Croix du Bonhomme, then descend into the Vallée des Glaciers. The exact start point is one of the details that will determine whether the day lands inside the mileage target.

  2. 02
    France → Italy · Les Chapieux → Courmayeur

    Cross the Col de la Seigne.

    Target: 14–16 mi with an optional trim · about 16.8 mi without it · +4,500–5,500 ft · 5–7 hr

    Run into Italy beneath the Mont Blanc massif. Altitude Montblanc publishes the complete reverse-direction Courmayeur–Les Chapieux stage at 27 km / 16.8 mi via Col de Chécrouit, so a short vehicle assist is a convenience rather than a requirement. Running the full connection puts the five-day trip near 59–65 miles. Finish in Courmayeur with a real shower, dinner and a private-room option.

  3. 03
    Italy · Courmayeur → Rifugio Bonatti

    The short day with the biggest view.

    Target: 9–10 mi · +3,500–4,500 ft · 3½–5 hr

    Climb out of Courmayeur and traverse the Italian side toward Val Ferret. A shorter mileage day leaves time to settle into Rifugio Bonatti, eat well and watch the light move across the Grandes Jorasses.

  4. 04
    Italy → Switzerland · Bonatti → La Fouly

    One last border, then wheels.

    Target: 13–14 mi · +3,000–4,000 ft · 4½–6½ hr

    Pass through the upper Val Ferret, cross Grand Col Ferret and descend to La Fouly. The non-negotiable logistics piece is a direct vehicle pickup immediately after the run, returning the group to the same Chamonix hotel that afternoon rather than adding another overnight on the trail.

  5. 05
    France · Flégère → Lac Blanc

    Finish high above Chamonix.

    Target: 5–7 mi · +2,000–3,000 ft · 3–4½ hr

    Use the Flégère lift to start high, then link a Lac Blanc and Lacs des Chéserys route before descending by lift. This is the celebratory day, but still rocky alpine terrain. Lift dates, hours and the final route must be checked for August 2027.

Working total: about 56–65 miles. With a small Day 2 trim, the original planning window remains about 56–64 miles. Running the full published 27 km / 16.8 mi connection gives a calculated range of about 59–65 miles. Only a measured GPX can confirm the final variants and climbing.

The hut night

One refuge, deliberately.

The plan keeps the high-mountain atmosphere without making every night a dormitory gamble. Bonatti is the exception because its location is part of the experience, not just somewhere to sleep.

That also makes it the first availability check. If Bonatti cannot work for the group’s dates and room requirements, the route needs to change before anything else is booked.

Who could coordinate it?

Supported self-guided is the promising middle.

Runcation appears open to flexible self-guided coordination based on our Dolomites conversation, so it is the first operator worth asking about this exact shape. That is potential, not confirmation: they have not agreed to this Mont Blanc route, price or August 2027 dates.

Altitude Montblanc offers fixed 5-, 6- and 7-day trail-running options, but directly declined a tailor-made itinerary because accommodation along the route is difficult to secure.

The useful next inquiry

Send Runcation the date window, group size, room/bathroom requirements, this five-day shape and the direct Day 4 transfer requirement. Ask for a feasibility check before asking for a detailed quote.
